This page describes two optional domain extensions as one architectural path:
the published `canwu-culture` culture system SDK and the implemented experimental
`canwu-law` legal institutionalization extension. Both build on the public API
and social diffusion simulation module, but neither puts historical culture,
legal semantics, or a particular period into the Canwu simulation core.

The governing rule is: **culture produces evidence-bearing social signals;
law interprets those signals through an authorized institutional procedure and
commits versioned law.** Cultural support is evidence, not legal authority, and
never becomes a statute automatically.

## Ownership and layering

`canwu-culture` is an authoring and compilation extension above
`canwu-society`. It accepts versioned reference content, validates cardinality,
expanded fan-out, memory, state, and boundary-work budgets, and produces an
externally immutable execution plan bound to a content hash.
`canwu-society` remains the runtime for sparse population distributions, social
influence edges, organization topology, institutional inputs, and
actor-relative projections.

The `canwu-law` extension is downstream from cultural signals. It owns
jurisdictions, legal institutions, procedures, proposals, enacted rules,
effective times, amendment, repeal, expiry, and legal interpretation. Political,
election, administration, education, justice, and enforcement extensions own
their own processes and consume enacted law at declared settlement boundaries.

```text
reference content pack
        |
        v
canwu-culture authoring and compiler
        |
        v
canwu-society sparse social runtime
        |
        +--> CulturalSignalBatch (bounded, causal, next-boundary ingress)
                    |
                    v
             canwu-law (proposal -> procedure -> LawVersion)
                    |
                    v
       political / election / administration / education / justice / enforcement adapters
```

The dependency direction is one way: information and correspondence may feed
culture; culture may emit generic signals; law may consume them. `canwu-core`,
`canwu-sim`, and `canwu-api` do not depend on legal semantics. Extensions use
canonical next-boundary ingress and bounded batches, never a synchronous event
bus, mutable callback, or cross-extension state write.

## Culture authoring contract

A reference content pack supplies an owned, serializable `CultureDefinition`.
A Rust builder and JSON/TOML loader should share one validator, so authored and
generated content have identical deterministic rules. A run rejects definitions
that exceed any cardinality, clause, fan-out, memory, state, or per-boundary
work budget before settlement begins.

### Definition components

| Component | Purpose | Contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Target | Idea, norm, movement, practice, school, or affiliation variant | Neutral profile defaults, ancestry, metadata, provenance, and lifecycle policy |
| Cohort | Aggregate population with territory and integer headcount | Application-defined classifications such as language, occupation, education, or status |
| Channel | Exposure or reinforcement opportunity | Reach, trust, interpretation fidelity, delay, capacity, and policy modifiers |
| Transition | Maps named signals to social state | Uses the existing awareness, private assent, practice, public alignment, organization tie, mobilization, and visibility dimensions |
| Institution and policy binding | Declares external decision pressure | Can change access, support, enforcement, censorship, disruption, or migration pressure, but never private assent directly |
| Effect binding | Declares an output for another extension | Signal kind, scope, cadence, persistence class, evidence, and bounded batch/fan-out |

Named traits and affinities may exist at the authoring boundary, but the runtime
compiles them into bounded rule factors or channel signals. It must not attach
an unbounded value map to every population bucket or create a second
per-person solver.

### Compiled plan and hot path

`CompiledCulturePlan` is compile-only and externally immutable for one
scenario/run revision. It contains interned numeric IDs; canonical sorted rule
tables and reverse indexes; compact channel, transition, effect, and institution
tables; scoped keys; lifecycle indexes; declared plan, state, and boundary
budgets; and a content hash. Changing a definition or compiled ordering creates
a new semantic plan revision instead of mutating an existing run.

## Incremental settlement and scale

Settlement is driven by a dirty set of active `(cohort, target)` pairs. An
admitted exposure, policy change, organization change, or reactivation marks
affected relationships. A transition boundary then:

1. consumes admitted signals in canonical order;
2. evaluates dirty pairs and bounded dependants;
3. updates aggregate counters incrementally;
4. refreshes projections only for observers that can see changed pairs; and
5. emits bounded effect batches for the next eligible consumer boundary.

For `D` active pairs, `Delta` dirty pairs, `B` buckets per pair, `E_delta`
affected edges, and `V_delta` affected observer entries, intended steady-state
work is approximately `O(Delta * B + E_delta + V_delta)`. Full plan rebuilds are
for definition changes, migration, or explicit maintenance. The current
full-state society path may remain a compatibility fallback, but new APIs must
not make full scans appear free.

## Culture lifecycle

Every target has an explicit generation and one of `Active`, `Dormant`, or
`Retired` state:

```text
Active -> Dormant -> Retired
             ^          |
             +----------+
        explicit reactivation creates a new generation
```

### Active and dormant

An `Active` target has engaged population, active propagation,
institutional/policy inputs, or a scheduled reactivation path. Its
distributions, rules, and projections are eligible for ordinary settlement.

A target becomes `Dormant` after a configured quiet window with no engaged
population and no admitted work. Engaged headcount is not the distribution
total: a neutral-only relationship does not keep a target alive. Dormancy
removes the target from culture hot and dirty indexes and, after explicit
society synchronization, stops its compiled culture transition rules. Existing
society distributions and a compact reactivation descriptor remain available.
Dormancy is reversible and does not erase history.

### Retirement and atomic synchronization

After its retention policy, a dormant target is eligible for `Retired` only
after all signals admitted for the boundary have been applied and no live
transition, organization, institution, policy, effect batch, admitted input, or
scheduled continuation requires its current generation.

Retirement writes a compact `RetiredTargetTombstone` with target identity and
generation, last active simulation time and revision, retirement reason and
policy hash, any explicit successor, and the evidence references needed for
replay and audit. It releases only rebuildable target-scoped society state.
Historical domain-record versions, events, actor knowledge, and archived
evidence remain queryable.

Hosts should use `settle_culture_society_boundary`: it prepares a bounded
runtime delta and stages society changes only when a lifecycle transition
occurs. A live external dependency rejects retirement before caller-owned state
changes. The host persists the culture record, society state, and typed
lifecycle transition in one authoritative transaction. The full
`synchronize_society_lifecycle` scan is for load repair and explicit maintenance
checkpoints.

Retirement never deletes another extension's live organization, policy,
influence edge, rule, or binding. New exposure for a retired generation is
rejected unless an explicit reactivation command or ingress is admitted.
Reactivation initializes only required active relationships, cites the old
tombstone, and creates a new generation; it does not rewrite history or revive
every former cohort silently.

## From cultural signal to law

Information and correspondence first resolve access and interpretation, then
may emit a bounded `CultureExposureSignalBatch`. Culture settlement updates
awareness, private assent, practice, public alignment, organization tie, and
mobilization, then emits a bounded `CulturalSignalBatch` containing target
generation, scope, strength, persistence class, cadence, and evidence. This
batch is a legal input, not an authority grant.

The legal bridge processes only dirty or due jurisdictions and proposals:

1. Reverse indexes map signal kind and scope to affected jurisdictions and open
   proposals; unrelated law is not scanned.
2. Typed `LegalMutation` and holder-context ingress enter the event-driven law
   plugin. It checks the embedded compiled-plan binding, exact directory and shard versions,
   host-owned `expected_versions`, cited culture generation, exact compiled
   signal-provider `(plugin, packet_type)`, and the kernel-committed producing
   boundary. Direct host injection into the provider namespace is not evidence.
   It does not trust a caller-declared signal kind. Publicity events additionally
   match the retained provider payload to the exact proposal, occurrence time,
   medium, and scope; generic practice signals use identity and producer-boundary
   evidence.
3. A proceeding creates a holder-bound decision outbox item. The adapter first
   persists its expected revision, registers an exact seat controller at most
   once, and submits ticket-open requests that reuse it. ACK requires the needed
   outcomes to be `Accepted` and the current controller/ticket to match the
   persisted draft exactly; ingress archival does not invalidate this proof.
4. An authorized controller selects an existing option. The accepted command
   can only schedule a bounded pending legal intent; it cannot write law.
5. A later law-plugin boundary revalidates competence, procedure, revision and
   effective-time guards, clause/evidence limits, and the culture generation,
   then atomically commits the owner-scoped legal shard bundle. Downstream adapters
   consume the result at their own declared boundary.

If the seat controller does not exist, `canwu-law` creates the default Human
controller. A host may instead pre-register the same stable controller ID with
Utility, Rule, Random, External, or LLM policy. The law adapter preserves that
policy when authority, seat, permission profile, and command subject exactly match
the compiled plan. Random produces decision ingress through an operation-keyed
boundary draw; External/LLM returns one existing option ID through the strict
selector DTO. Neither can change legal options or authority.

```text
CulturalSignalBatch
  -> LegalProposal / DecisionTicket
  -> authorized DecisionAttempt / DecisionTrace
  -> accepted legal command -> pending intent
  -> atomic legal shard bundle commit
  -> LawVersion
  -> downstream enforcement and feedback evidence
```

No synchronous cross-extension callback is allowed. A consumer that cannot
accept a batch records a rejection or defers it; it never partially mutates
culture or law.

## Legal records and procedure

`LegalJurisdiction` binds a stable jurisdiction ID to its parent, territory
scope, competent institutions, and procedure profile. `LegalInstitution` binds
an institution entity to a jurisdiction, authority seats, quorum, vote or
appointment rules, and the command subject allowed to adopt law. Both are
members of the typed legal shard state, not new core entity kinds or
independently mutable host records.

Compilation requires every procedure seat to resolve to exactly one institution
that declares both the procedure and seat. Its holder, permission profile, and
collision-free length-prefixed controller ID are frozen into the plan; missing
or ambiguous authority fails before the run starts.

`LegalProposal` is a non-enacted, versioned proceeding input. It records the
jurisdiction, sponsor and subjects, bounded typed clauses or eligibility rules,
required procedure and deadline, status (`draft`, `submitted`, `deliberating`,
`adopted`, `rejected`, `expired`, or `withdrawn`), signal and evidence
references, the open ticket identity and option version, and its claimed legal
competence, defects, validity, and exact origin. Kernel authorization proves who
submitted the command; it does not make an in-world ultra vires act valid. A
target generation may be cited as evidence but never receives permission to
mutate or enact the proposal.

Institutional competence is default-deny across legal order, jurisdiction,
subject matter, source mode, operation, procedure, forum, and adjudicative
power. Each source profile also declares procedural-institution or
evidence-claim authority, exact origin and publicity policies, a compiled
publicity signal provider, evidence bounds,
claimant rules, and retrospective permission. `Promulgated` is always
procedural; `Agreed` compiles an exact instrument kind, at least two parties,
and ratification evidence. A publicity event is admitted only on the boundary
where its provider ingress actually occurs; a future publication plan is not an
occurred event.

An accepted proposal creates one immutable `LegalSourceVersion`, the stable
`LegalRule`, and one immutable `LawVersion`. The source keeps the exact proposal
and ruling, agreement, or reception origin; its explicit mode is
`Promulgated`, `Adjudicated`, `Accreted`, `Agreed`, or `Received`. The rule owns
the latest claim separately from the operative version, so a `Purported` or
`Contested` change remains visible while the prior valid version still governs.
Publication is a separate immutable `LegalPublicityEvent` bound to the exact
proposal, time, medium, scope, and evidence. A validity condition rejects
adoption without it. An effectiveness condition may accept the proposal first,
but the new version remains inert and absent from historical read cuts before
publication, which must occur no later than the effective time. Delayed
publication appends the event and updates proposal lifecycle plus derived rule
state without rewriting the create-only source or law version. Retroactive
effect additionally requires both profile permission and an explicit
retrospective date.

`LawVersion` records a stable rule ID and monotonic legal ordinal, jurisdiction
and bounded scope, effective times, source and origin, causal evidence, and
predecessor links. Every compiled clause declares its normative modality rather
than deriving it from display text. Rights and eligibility rules include
holders, duty bearers, subject matter, conditions, standing, forum, and remedy
profile. Amendment and repeal are new legal commands and append-only versions.
An enacted law does not disappear when its cultural target retires.

Applicability is a typed, bounded query over an exact legal order and compiled
profile. It filters time, territory, persons, subject matter, and jurisdiction;
applies compiled condition/exception predicates and precedence; and returns
governing versions, displaced claims, conflicts, and a trace. Missing predicate
facts return `Indeterminate`; a false condition or true exception returns
`NotApplicable`. Every fact carries exact evidence. Actor-relative reads bind a
`KnowledgeReadCut` and holder record per fact; the host-bound API verifies the
holder, complete cut, compiled knowledge schema, JSON boolean pointer, asserted
value, and evidence. The detached API rejects actor-relative queries.
Unresolved validity returns `Contested`, including the
prior operative version and its rival claim. Succession does not inherit a
predecessor order by default: reception uses the longest matching rule prefix
within the succession's declared personal and territorial scope, then applies
the received rule's own subject-matter scope. `Continue` exposes
the predecessor directly; `Transform` and `Review` require an explicit
`Receive` source bound to the exact succession and predecessor. `Transform`
also binds and uses one compiled target clause.

Jurisdiction queries use relation-kind/direction adjacency compiled once and
build one candidate-budgeted reachable set. A total query-work budget also
covers graph edges, rules, historical versions, nested effect/holder/predicate
visits, conflict-index fan-out, and conflict members. A separate per-record
nested-item budget is checked before traversing proposals, cases, rulings, or
conflicts. A resolved conflict stores exact total, governing, and
displaced version sets with a typed basis, jurisdiction, recorded time,
effective interval, and rationale. Its complete claim set and spatiotemporal
scope must match before the partition may promote or displace a claim;
non-temporal resolution requires an operative, case-bound competent ruling whose
version sets match and whose scope covers an explicit conflict jurisdiction.
All active partitions merge simultaneously; contradictory governing and
displaced sets remain `Contested` rather than depending on conflict IDs.
Cases, findings, and rulings are also constrained by compiled forum, proof,
standing, remedy, precedent, interval, issue, and adjudicative competence.

### Effect persistence semantics

| Culture effect | Legal interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| `Pulse` | Opens or updates a proposal opportunity; no durable law exists yet. |
| `Level` | Supplies current support or legitimacy pressure; its end may trigger review, never automatic repeal. |
| `Commitment` | Once accepted by legal command, provides provenance for durable `LawVersion`; culture retirement cannot retract it. |
| `Evidence` | Historical citation only; it cannot open or mutate a proceeding directly. |

When a legal rule intentionally depends on a live cultural level, the legal
extension records that dependency and owns its review, expiry, or renewal rule.
Culture emits the end of the level; it does not silently repeal law. A
commitment accepted into law does not keep a culture target hot, so the target
may retire while the law remains active.
The active law keeps only a compact, Merkle-bound adoption-evidence receipt.
The retired culture target, propagation indexes, and old payload leave the hot
path; repeal or expiry remains an explicit legal operation.

Retirement is explicit bounded maintenance. Format 8 keeps target-keyed culture
dependency records and requires the culture owner plus every registered
dependency resolver to contribute an owner-scoped proposal. The kernel
preflights the complete set against one persistent domain root and commits all
participants or none. Missing proposals, cross-owner writes, stale versions,
and budget overflow fail before culture or legal state changes; ordinary legal
settlement never scans total legal history to prove retirement safety.

## Authority, visibility, and replay

Legal commands use the ordinary authority chain:

```text
legal facts -> DecisionTicket -> controller selection
  -> DecisionAttempt / DecisionTrace
  -> canonical command ingress
  -> legal authority and procedure validation
  -> LawVersion commit
```

The command subject must be the proposal's exact frozen subject; separate
compiled competence determines in-world legal power. Its context carries
the validated controller ID, decision origin, seat, permission profile, request
identity, and expected revision/time guards. An external service or model may
recommend an option, but cannot manufacture authority or submit a raw law
payload that bypasses the ticket.

Public enacted law may be exposed through a domain projection. Private drafts,
dissent, and actor-specific knowledge use `ViewerContext` and the holder ledger;
there is no truth fallback. Culture and legal records implement
`DomainRecordType` with strict schemas, typed references, explicit mutation
policies, and retained version bodies where evidence needs exact historical
meaning.

Format 8 persists independently versioned plan, directory, order/jurisdiction
shard, coordinator, culture-dependency, and archive-head records. Load admits
only the declared working set and reconstructs derived indexes, authenticated
archive roots, and hot projections before exposing state. Exact replay consumes
mutation, signal, decision, command, ACK, and wake ingress plus boundary
evidence. It never reruns
a human, service, or model policy. Forks copy validated state and continue with
new causal inputs. Failed boundaries restore indexes, tombstones, counters,
evidence, and random positions atomically.

Full history and derived-index validation runs at cold load or restore. Live
boundaries check immutable plan/budget bindings and local mutation guards rather
than scanning the complete legal history once per mutation. A latest live
disputed claim retains its identity evidence until a replacement supersedes it.

## Bounded work and conformance

The legal plan compiles numeric IDs for jurisdictions, institutions, proposals,
clauses, and procedure profiles; reverse indexes by signal kind and scope;
dirty proposal and jurisdiction sets; per-procedure limits for clauses,
evidence, options, fan-out, and pending continuations; and a plan hash and
budget manifest. For `P_delta` dirty proposals, `C_delta` affected clauses, and
`V_delta` observer entries, intended steady-state work is approximately
`O(P_delta + C_delta + V_delta)`. Deadline and effective-time wakes use ordered
indexes, exact decision-result proof uses a request index, and both deleted and
inserted applicability rows consume the mutation budget. Retired targets and
historical law catalogs must not increase active proposal settlement cost.

That complexity describes shard-local semantic work. Format 8 uses persistent
HAMT/Patricia structures, content-addressed page deltas, authenticated legal
archive indexes, and root-only cold storage. In the 2026-08-30 release probe,
50 ordinary dirty-shard boundaries with 100k and 1M cold legal records measured
6.558/11.302ms and 6.920/11.733ms median/p95 respectively; cold history remained
outside the ordinary boundary curve. The real one-million-record archive index
uses 65,536 membership, 65,536 effective-time, and 65,535 recorded-time pages.
Its largest page has 37 entries and 37,335 canonical bytes, with one candidate
per sampled sparse point query. The full build uses 245 production archive
batches and never retains more than 4,096 hot candidates. Boundary proposal
validation applies small overlays to structurally shared roots and validates
only the affected reference closure; empty stages do not materialize untouched
records. A separate selector stress probe with one million candidates examines
and materializes only the selected 4,096-record batch. Its 2.49-GB shard is
deliberately outside the live 128-MiB legal state/memory contract. The real
Canwu probe used an admissible 16,384-candidate, 38.40-MiB persisted shard: the
empty boundary measured 0.169ms, the unrelated legal boundary 2.563ms, and the
candidate record version remained unchanged.

Archiving changes historical payload placement, not current law, and culture
retirement does not automatically repeal enacted human-rights or voting
eligibility rules. After restart, the unified GC reachability manifest still
marks legal content/blob/index pages and decision locator/blob IDs. Dense
same-time temporal cells are split into ordered authenticated segments while
each page remains capped at 64 entries and 1 MiB. Pending verified maintenance
ingress also retains its exact objects, transferring them to committed roots on
apply or releasing them after terminal stale rejection. A missing provider
reports explicit unavailability rather than treating unknown history as absent.
Every canonical archive result remains attributable to its retention handle
until the host completes store finalization. Finalization derives the recorded
disposition from the reloaded authoritative runtime and queues a private
acknowledgement that retires that recovery record. The synchronous helper
authenticates the complete store-bound head and applies on a clone. Paged
decision restore likewise loads only the exact archived ticket/trace locator
pages still referenced by hot decision objects before validation.

Conformance evidence should prove that:

- cultural signals cannot directly mutate legal records;
- only a controller-bound authorized command can enact, amend, or repeal law;
- stale proposal, ticket, and law revisions become safe persisted rejections;
- a commitment accepted into law survives cultural retirement;
- an unresolved live-level dependency blocks culture retirement;
- a future-effective live-level dependency also blocks early retirement;
- expired procedures expire unresolved pending/enqueued outbox work;
- proposal, law, evidence, archive, snapshot, fork, and exact replay remain
  consistent; and
- unrelated targets, observers, and retired catalog entries do not perturb
  keyed results or declared work budgets.

A women's suffrage content pack might emit public-alignment, organization-
capacity, and legitimacy-pressure signals. A competent assembly selects a
voting-eligibility option through `DecisionTicket`; an accepted command schedules
an intent that a later legal boundary commits as a `VotingEligibilityRule` with
a deterministic effective date; and the election adapter reads the law. "Adult
women" must be defined by the content pack's historical context: citizenship,
race, property, marital status, colonial status, and registration rules can
create intersecting exclusions. Retiring the culture target later stops new
propagation while the enacted rule and enforcement history remain intact.

An Enlightenment and human-rights content pack should likewise avoid a linear
story in which European ideas automatically become modern rights. It can model
plural intellectual roots, translation, print, education, and association as
distinct transmission networks, alongside countermobilization by religious,
dynastic, local, and incumbent interests. Those signals may change public
alignment, organizational capacity, legitimacy pressure, and decision evidence;
different legal orders can still adopt only part of a claim, reinterpret it,
delay publicity, enforce it selectively, or reject it.
