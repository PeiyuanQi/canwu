The [military simulation extension](/en/reference/terminology/), `canwu-military`, is an **engagement engine**. It resolves fights between typed forces at a place your world owns, under a **combat rulebook** your game installs and a **step resolver**; what it writes is records and signals that your game applies. It owns no doctrine, unit catalogue, terrain table, weather, control or occupation state, store, or report text. Read this page when you decide whether to drive your combat with it, or how to fit your own combat model into it.

In brief: every number that decides a fight is a parameter of your rulebook. Your game places forces and supplies their condition each day. The engine steps the fight, records every per-step quantity, and closes it into an `engagement_summary` record; a fallen place is a fact in that summary, never an occupation the engine creates.

## Core model

- **Combat rulebook** (`CombatRulebookV1`, 战斗规则册): step profiles, per-kind rules for the six engagement kinds (`Field`, `Assault`, `Sortie`, `Relief`, `FerryFight`, `BridgeheadFight`), forecast terms and bands, the four lines, cohesion, morale, break, pursuit, terrain, night, fortification, siege and crossing rules, and the limits of every collection the engine keeps. It is hashed; the scenario installs it, or a `System` issuer sends `InstallRulebook`. A run holds one.
- **Force** (`ForceStateV1`): a force stands at a place your world owns (`LocationV1::At { site }`); your game places it with `PlaceForce` when it arrives. Its components carry the values your game compiles from its unit profiles; its side terms carry cohesion cap, morale break, battle command, assessment, credibility, and traits.
- **Engagement** (`EngagementStateV1`, 交战): two sides at a site, each with its forces, its report holders, its latest **condition input** (`ConditionInputV1`, 战斗条件输入), the battle's morale shock, cohesion, and losses; a **pre-battle forecast** (`ForecastV1`, 战前预判) from the rulebook's terms and ratio bands.
- **Engagement step** (`StepV1`, 战斗步): one resolution of the step formula with every per-step quantity, the inputs' digests, the draws, and the resolver's identity.
- **Siege** (`SiegeStateV1`, 围城) and **contested crossing** (`CrossingStateV1`, 渡河作战): day-grained and wave-grained processes that open their own engagements.

<Steps>
1. Your game sends a command; admission checks its shape and its report holders and queues an apply packet for the next boundary at the same instant.
2. A fight steps only in a boundary that admits a step packet. With `with_step_cadence(Some(cadence))` (daily by default) the wake system schedules one whenever work is due, stepped in the next boundary at the same instant; with `with_step_cadence(None)` your game sends `Step { up_to }` after its inputs, stepped in the boundary that applies them. Either way your game may instead queue a timed step (`timed_step_ingress(due_at)`) for a boundary it settles anyway, such as the next daily one: that boundary steps, and stepping adds no boundary of its own.
3. In phase 7 the settle system applies the boundary's commands in admission order, then steps every engagement, siege day, and crossing wave due by then.
4. Each step builds the resolver's input from the sides' components, their latest inputs, the environment, the kind's rule, and the site; calls the resolver; splits casualties over components by largest remainder (each component takes the whole part of its proportional share, and the places left over go to the largest fractional remainders); applies the break rule and pursuit; and appends the step.
5. A side that withdraws or routs closes the engagement; its `max_minutes` without a break (the kind's, or a shorter time your game named when it opened the fight) closes it `Stalled`. The close writes a create-only `engagement_summary` record and, if you asked for one, sends the outcome signal.
6. In phase 13 the report system publishes each side's holder-relative report within the knowledge budget.
</Steps>

### The step and the resolver

The resolver is a type: `MilitaryPlugin<R: StepResolver>`. `ParametricStepResolver` computes four lines per side (fire, shock, melee, and defense: one integer term each) from the components, terrain, night, fortification, and supply, and a per-step rate each side inflicts; every per-step quantity (inflicted share, cohesion loss, morale shock) scales that rate once by the profile's `step_scale_bp` (in basis points, hundredths of a percent), so a daily profile can compress many short steps into one. A game with its own formula implements `StepResolver` once and may ask for keyed draws through `StepDraws`. Activation refuses a rulebook that names another resolver than the plugin runs.

The engine owns everything around the call: it clamps the outcome to the rulebook's ranges, applies the break rule (a defender behind closed walls of the rulebook's grade never breaks), applies pursuit on a rout, and decides whether the fight continues. Step profiles let one fight run on a daily step while another is promoted to half-hour steps (`ReviseStepProfile`).

### Sieges and crossings

A siege day computes investment from the besiegers' men and any naval control, emplaces guns and then bombards the walls toward the grade's breach threshold, and opens an assault when ordered or, with `storm_on: BreachOrGate`, the day a breach or a gate opens. A sortie opens when ordered and the defenders' cooldown has passed; a relief is an engagement you open against the besiegers. Defenders breaking at the wall make the siege fall and disperse the defenders still there; besiegers routed lift it. A second column joins an invested place with `JoinSiege`, and enters on its side any assault or sortie under way; `ReviseSiege { release }` lets a force on either side that is not fighting leave, so you can place it elsewhere. A siege whose last defender leaves falls `Abandoned` at its next siege day, and `LiftSiege` ends one whose last besieger leaves.

A crossing sends one wave per wave interval. An interceptor whose control is high enough catches a wave by threshold or by a keyed draw; a caught wave fights a ferry fight and turns back; a landed wave may fight a bridgehead fight against the defenders on the far bank, which the order names and `ReviseCrossing { defenders }` revises when a force reaches that bank later. Your game moves the force when the crossing completes.

### When a command fails

A command that fails admission is a rejected command attempt. A command that fails in phase 7 (an unknown or engaged force, a stale `expected_host_revision`, an issuer who controls no force, a limit, an unknown posture or profile) is recorded with a `canwu.military.command_rejected.v1` event whose summary begins with the error code. A step the engine cannot take (an oversized step, a failing resolver, too many draws) holds its engagement with `canwu.military.engagement_held.v1` until a `SupplyInput`, `ReviseStepProfile`, or `OrderAction { Abandon }`. None of these fails the boundary.

## Knowledge and visibility

Each side names its report holders: people, governments, or the force records themselves. Holders are checked when the command is admitted and again when a report is published, so a holder who has died is skipped. Progress reports (`engagement_report`) supersede the side's last; closing reports (`engagement_outcome`) are kept. A fight, siege, or crossing whose sides name no holder queues no report, so a game that writes its own reports from the records pays the report system nothing. A side sees its own losses exactly and the enemy's as bands from the rulebook's cuts.

## Randomness, persistence, and replay

Two random streams, `crossing-interception` and `step-contingency`, are drawn by keyed address with the admitted step packet as evidence, so a live, sealed, restored, and replayed run draw alike. Every command that changes a record names that record's `expected_host_revision`, which only host commands advance: a step in the same instant never makes your command stale. The wake cadence, the signal target, and the publication reservation are part of the plugin descriptor, so a run restores and replays only with the same choices.

## What your application supplies

- The rulebook, compiled from your content.
- Forces and their revisions (muster, desertion, wounded returns) and their placement on arrival.
- Engagements, sieges, and crossings opened from your own contact rules, with each side's report holders.
- One condition input per side and one environment input a day, derived from your supply, pay, command, and weather records, cited as evidence.
- Your own rules the engine does not decide: betrayal (`ReviseSiege { gate_open_steps }`), storming doctrine, rations, naval patrols, promotion of a fight.
- What follows a fight: control, occupation, population, stores, and player text, from the summary or the outcome signal.

## Limits and budgets

Every collection the engine keeps has a cap in the rulebook's limits: forces, components per force, forces and holders per side, open engagements, sieges, and crossings, step bytes, draws per step, and the report queue. A boundary without a step packet does no stepping work; a stepping boundary costs in proportion to the open engagements and the forces in them. A closed fight leaves about 2 KB of records for the life of the run. The engine has two sides per engagement, no automatic contact, and banded enemy figures in its reports.
