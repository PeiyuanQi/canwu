The engine settles commands, schedules work, and records evidence, but it has no
rules of its own for trade, war, or taxes. Domain systems supply those rules.
Most of them are domain extensions: optional crates built on `canwu-api` that you
add as dependencies and register as plugins. Register only the systems your
application needs; the others take no part in the run. First-party extensions
use the same plugin interface as the plugins you write, so you can replace any
of them.

A few systems also provide shared types that `canwu-api` re-exports: knowledge
ledgers, decision tickets, route plans, and transport records.

Each system has a design page that covers what it owns, how it runs inside a
boundary, and what your application supplies. Routing, military, and
technology also have walkthroughs:
[route planning and delivery](/en/tutorials/routing-transport/),
[military extension](/en/tutorials/military-domain/), and
[technology diffusion](/en/tutorials/technology-diffusion/).

## Scenarios that combine systems

Most games use several systems at once. The [case studies](/en/tutorials/cases/)
run complete scenarios, such as grain supply for an army or a letter routed from
Wuxi to Beijing, and show how the systems hand records to one another.
