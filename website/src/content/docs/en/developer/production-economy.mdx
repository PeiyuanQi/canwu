Canwu 0.11.0 provides optional resource and production extensions, but it does not put grain, factories, prices, historical resource deposits, or armies into the simulation core.

## Recommended boundary

- The simulation core owns command admission, deterministic time, phase ordering, atomic commit, rollback, visibility, persistence, and replay.
- `canwu-resource` owns resource/unit revisions, `ResourceAccount`, protected floors, `ResourceDemand`, `ResourceReservation`, exact allocation legs, transfer, consumption, loss, and fulfillment. It is the sole physical-balance writer.
- `canwu-production` owns processes, production sites, facilities, capacity, work orders, work in progress, execution, projects, and output settlement. It consumes exact resource outcomes and technology evidence without keeping a second inventory.
- `canwu-transport` owns itinerary, capacity, legs, execution, custody handoffs, and delivery completion. The resource extension owns cargo escrow balance, loss, acceptance, return, and destination credit.
- Population and military domains form demand and consequences separately. They cannot directly rewrite resource balances or production state.

These optional extensions sit above the public `canwu-api`; they do not become reverse dependencies of the simulation core. Host games still own money, markets, wages, building UI, combat formulas, and historical content.

## Three terms to understand first

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Protected floor | The minimum amount an account must retain instead of offering it to the current allocation |
| Resource reservation | A `ResourceReservation` bound to an exact demand, account, resource/unit revision, and lifecycle; failure or rollback cannot leave a hidden hold |
| Custody predecessor | The exact prior record that authorizes the next cargo-state transition and prevents duplicate credit or material creation |

## Minimal lifecycle

```text
Demand -> resource reservation/allocation -> source debit -> in-transit custody
       -> arrival acceptance -> destination credit -> next-boundary consumption
```

1. Live demands are submitted and amended through holder-authorized resource commands. Typed adapter ingress supports only its declared provider operations, not demand submission or amendment; consumers never write stock directly.
2. `canwu-resource` deterministically allocates against protected floors, minimum useful amounts, partial-fulfillment policies, due times, and stable tie-break keys.
3. Production, force, and other consumers cite exact allocation and fulfillment evidence and write only their own work-order or consequence records.
4. Conservation, version, custody-predecessor, or authority failure rolls back the whole proposal group.
5. A destination is credited only after the cargo disposition is accepted; military consumption becomes eligible at the next appropriate boundary.

The account amount is the only writable quantity. Reserved, available, and protected views are derived; reach is not delivery and arrival is not consumption.

## Authorize a custom consumer

Version 0.11.0 supports custom consumption providers using the existing
`ResourceConsumptionIntentV1` contract. Keep a top-level
`resource_consumption_intents` map in your plugin's owned, active domain record;
each key is the sealed intent's `id`. No callback or reference-provider name is
needed. Register that record kind in `ResourcePlugin::new` so the adapter may
read the exact source.

Before submission, complete the existing allocation and completion-lease
lifecycle. Submit `ResourceOperationRequestV1::Consume` through canonical adapter
ingress. Its `consumer_evidence` must equal the exact **current** provider source;
the completion certificate must bind that source and the same operation key.
The holder, participant, time, revision and lease checks still apply.

Exactly one `Authorized` intent must match the complete allocation leg, demand
and account revisions, consumption identity and operation key. Partial-leg
consumption is outside this contract. The map may contain at most the resource
state's `max_operation_outcomes` entries; excessive maps fail before decoding.
Missing or malformed maps and identity/digest mismatches are rejected, as are
retired, ambiguous or mismatched authorizations. A sealed digest proves content
consistency, not permission by itself. Rejection does not authorize a debit.

Your provider owns authorization and retirement of the intent and any local
consequences after the resource receipt. `canwu-resource` owns the conserved
balance and duplicate-operation behavior. The force-supply reference retains
its specialized adapter, including its different retained-source policy; custom
consumers must use the current-source contract above.

## Versioning and saves

Hosts pin exact versions of `canwu-api`, `canwu-resource`, `canwu-production`, and plugin semantic hashes from the same release line. Loading restores the same Canwu snapshot and revalidates extension records; it must not rebuild an old campaign from its current timestamp or treat an unpublished local engine version as a compatible API.

## Demand source policy

Since 0.11.0, every `ResourceDemand` persists a `source_policy`:

- `ResourceDemandSourcePolicyV1::Pooled` preserves allocation across all open accounts with matching resource and unit revisions, in deterministic account-ID order.
- `ExactAccounts(Vec<ResourceAccountId>)` lists 1–256 accounts in strictly increasing ID order, with no duplicates. Each must exist, be open, match both exact revisions, and have the demand requester as its custodian. Invalid lists reject the command before resources change; allocation validates them again.

The allocator computes available supply, minimum useful quantity and partial fulfillment from the selected accounts only. An insufficient exact list never falls back to the pool. Existing protected floors and later transfer/consumption authority checks still apply. The exact path visits only the listed accounts, bounded by `MAX_DEMAND_SOURCE_ACCOUNTS`; pooled selection retains its existing account scan.

A demand may change its policy before its first allocation, with the expected revision. Once it has any reservation, including a consumed reservation backing in-flight transfer escrow, or any fulfillment, its policy is fixed. Cancel the demand and submit a new one for a new policy; cancellation does not cancel a separate transfer. Reservation history remains indexed until the demand is terminal and its archive closure is eligible; terminal demands cannot be amended.

`Pooled` is a selection policy, not permission to spend another custodian's stock. A host application must control who may submit pooled demands and which requester it uses. Cross-custodian appointments, delegation, purpose and spending limits remain application/domain responsibilities; an account-ID list proves none of them. Transfer custody and completion-lease checks remain separate requirements.

The policy is included in request digests, runtime snapshots, exact replay and terminal demand archive payloads. Restore validates live source references and retained reservation membership; archive validation preserves the policy and its digest. Holder-relative reports retain their existing visibility contract and do not expose the authoritative source list automatically. Missing `source_policy` fields deserialize as `Pooled` in a standalone DTO, but this does not migrate old snapshots: exact engine-version and plugin-identity checks still apply. Rust struct literals must add the field; this is a 0.11.0 source break.
