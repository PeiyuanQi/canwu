This tutorial runs the military reference starter. An army marches with the reference world's movement plugin, the host places its force on arrival, and a field battle is stepped day by day and then in half-hour steps; meanwhile a place is besieged, breached, and stormed, and a river force crosses past an enemy fleet. You will learn how `canwu-military` takes your rulebook and your inputs, when it steps a fight, and which consequences it leaves to your game.

The [military simulation extension](/en/reference/terminology/) is split into three crates:

- `canwu-military`: the engagement engine: forces, engagements, sieges, crossings, their records and reports, built on `canwu-api`.
- `canwu-military-reference-content`: one explicitly synthetic combat rulebook.
- `canwu-military-reference`: a replaceable reference integration that installs that rulebook and six forces on the [reference world](/en/tutorials/move-army/).

## Run it

From the repository root:

```shell
cargo run -p canwu-military-reference --example military_starter
```

```text
engagement=canwu:military:crossing:capital-ford/ferry-0 kind=FerryFight outcome=Stalled steps=1 site_fell=false losses=0/0
engagement=canwu:military:engagement:eastern-field kind=Field outcome=AttackerPrevailed steps=121 site_fell=false losses=16/424
engagement=canwu:military:siege:westford/assault-1 kind=Assault outcome=AttackerPrevailed steps=5 site_fell=true losses=10/100
siege=Fell cause=Some(Breach) crossing=Complete landed=2000
…
military_starter=complete engagements=3 signals=3 checkpoint=…
```

`military_starter=complete` means every assertion passed: each closed fight signalled the host, the besieged place fell, and the restored and replayed copies have the same checkpoint hash as the live run. The lines between print the reports one government holds.

The starter's rulebook trusts the reference host's `System` issuer. Every command is a `MilitaryCommandV1` sent through canonical ingress with `military_command()`; a command that changes a record names that record's `expected_host_revision`. The engine steps only in a boundary that admits its step packet: here the default wake system schedules one on each daily calendar boundary that has work due. A host that prefers to drive stepping itself builds `MilitaryPlugin::new().with_step_cadence(None)` and sends `Step { up_to }` after its inputs.

## What the starter does

`demo_engagement_scenario()` starts from the reference world's demo scenario, adds a rival government, and installs `synthetic_rulebook_v1()` with six forces: a force at the capital that matches the field army, a rival field force in the east, a siege pair in the west, and a river force facing a rival fleet at the capital.

| Day | Host commands | What the engine does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | the army's movement order (reference world); `InvestSite` at Westford with `storm_on: BreachOrGate`; `OrderCrossing` from the capital toward Westford past the fleet | opens the siege and the crossing |
| 1 | `PlaceForce` for the arrived army; `OpenEngagement { kind: Field }` in the east | the first wave is intercepted into a ferry fight; the siege's guns emplace |
| 2 | `SupplyInput` for both sides; `ReviseCrossing { night: true }` | the field fight steps; the ferry fight closes; the night wave lands |
| 3 | `SupplyInput`; `ReviseStepProfile` to half-hour steps | the breach opens and the storm begins the same day |
| 4 and later | `SupplyInput` while the fight lasts | the field fight closes in half-hour steps; the assault breaks the garrison, the place falls, and its defenders disperse |

The field engagement, as the starter opens it:

```rust
MilitaryCommandV1::OpenEngagement(Box::new(OpenEngagementV1 {
    id: field(),
    kind: EngagementKind::Field,
    profile: None,
    site: place(ids.eastern_territory),
    fort: None,
    attackers: vec![force_id("yun-field")],
    defenders: vec![force_id("rival-field")],
    holders: [
        vec![ours.clone(), KnowledgeHolderRef::Person(ids.commander)],
        vec![theirs.clone()],
    ],
    inputs: [condition("field", 720), condition("camp", 680)],
    environment: environment(false),
}))
```

The host sends the `PlaceForce` and the `OpenEngagement` in one admission order: the engine checks co-location against the force as placed, so the two compose in the same boundary.

## What stays with your game

The engine writes only its own records. A fallen place is `site_fell` in the assault's summary and `Fell` with cause `Breach` on the siege; the starter's host receives each closed fight's `EngagementOutcomeSignalV1` in a tiny plugin of its own. Control and occupation, casualties as people, and supply stores are your game's to change from those facts; doctrine, betrayal, rations, and naval patrols are your game's to decide and pass in. [Military](/en/architecture/military/) lists what each command checks and writes, the step formula, and the limits.

## The reference rulebook is synthetic

`synthetic_rulebook_v1()` has six terrain rows, five fortification grades, five band cuts, four postures, a day and a half-hour step profile, the six kind rules, and the reference limits. Its values are demonstration numbers chosen so the starter's fights end in a few days; they make no historical claim. A game compiles its own rulebook from its own content.

Read the full [starter source](https://github.com/PeiyuanQi/canwu/blob/main/crates/integrations/canwu-military-reference/examples/military_starter.rs) for every command and the snapshot and replay checks.
