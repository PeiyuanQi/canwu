Canwu's military capability is an optional **domain extension**, not a new simulation-core subsystem. `canwu-military` provides military domain records, domain commands, boundary systems, draw evidence, and actor-relative projections through `canwu-api`; `canwu-military-reference-content` provides versioned rules and content; and `canwu-military-reference` provides a replaceable reference integration.

## 13 capabilities

The extension supports one persisted, exactly replayable causal chain:

1. Establish army: create formations, subunits, and lifecycle state.
2. Assign generals: maintain the command chain, authority, and command delay.
3. Recruit: connect society candidates with enlistment, training, and equipment.
4. March: use actor-relative route planning and delegate execution to transport.
5. Encounter: record contact, visibility, and deterministic arbitration.
6. Ambush: record concealment, preparation, trigger, and counter-observation.
7. Tactics and strategy: bind operational plans and choices to `DecisionTicket`.
8. Battle settlement: settle rounds, casualties, prisoners, retreat, and pursuit in stages.
9. Behind-enemy operations: support reconnaissance, raids, sabotage, infiltration, extraction, and return.
10. Supply: submit typed demand and receive reservation, transport, and shortage results.
11. Occupation control: manage object control, garrison, security, resistance, and governance prerequisites.
12. Assimilation: advance staged legal, social, and cultural integration signals.
13. Military administration: record military-government offices, policy, and fiscal/legal procedure.

## Ownership boundaries

The military extension is the sole writer for military force, operation, combat, occupation, and military-knowledge records. The simulation core owns time, canonical ingress, authority, scheduling, atomic commit, persistence, exact replay, and random evidence. Route planning and reachability belong to `canwu-routing`; movement, custody, capacity, and handoff belong to `canwu-transport`; resource accounts, reservations, consumption, and fulfillment belong to `canwu-resource`; population, law, fiscal procedure, information, and knowledge remain owned by their respective extensions. Military submits typed requests and binds exact receipts; it does not mutate another domain's state.

External effects first become provider-owned pending intent, reservation, or saga state. The provider settles them in its own canonical boundary and returns a source-bound acknowledgement through ingress. Queries, command errors, events, and reports must not disclose another actor's military ground truth.

## Synthetic-content disclaimer

The reference content pack's `riverine-preindustrial` and `industrial-front` profiles are explicitly **synthetic**. They exercise different organization, transport, communication, and replenishment assumptions; they are not historical facts, historical force estimates, or source-calibrated data.

## Example command

The host application submits a typed domain command through the public API, such as `OrderMarch`, instead of mutating a force record directly:

```text
military.submit(OrderMarch {
  force_id: force_17,
  destination: node_42,
  expected_revision: 8,
  operation_key: "march-17-42"
})
```

The command passes authority, revision, idempotency, route-snapshot, and boundary validation. Transport returns a provider receipt, and military state produces projections only from the committed boundary view.
