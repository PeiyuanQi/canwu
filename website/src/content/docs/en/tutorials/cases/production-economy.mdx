This case does not transplant Victoria 3's market model into Canwu. Its useful
design vocabulary is inputs, outputs, production methods, resource conditions,
and logistics constraints. Canwu supplies lower-level replaceable deterministic
contracts; CM remains responsible for markets, money, wages, building UI, and
combat formulas.

## How production happens

Production is not “the technology is unlocked, therefore a building emits goods
each week.” A run must satisfy an evidence chain:

```text
revisioned process
  + available production site and facility
  + materials, labor/capability, tools/machines, and energy
  + local qualification or installed technology implementation
  + authorization, season/environment, security, and access
  -> authorized work order and reserved capacity
  -> inputs become work in progress
  -> production execution
  -> outputs settle through the resource extension
```

Supplementary household work, distributed workshops, government workshops,
concentrated plants, and multi-site enterprises are revisioned data forms, not
levels on one universal building ladder. A game may present them as buildings;
the engine requires exact, replayable bindings for inputs, capability,
facilities, time, and output.

When a scenario needs slow diffusion or adoption, Canwu does not merely apply
an “era speed” penalty. It separates knowing a technique, qualifying to perform
it at a site, installing tools or facilities, adopting it for a particular use,
and maintaining access to inputs and routes. Any missing link becomes a
structured work-order blocker; a calendar year never grants capability.

## How geography affects production

Canwu does not use a timeless `has_coal = true` flag. Reference content uses
effective-dated, source-cited capability stages:

```text
potential -> observed/surveyed -> proven -> technically extractable
          -> operating site -> route accessible -> delivered and accepted
```

Geological presence is not usable production. Extraction process,
authorization, labor, equipment, energy, security, road/water/rail access, and
destination acceptance all constrain supply. Routing proposes a plan;
transport records itinerary, capacity, execution, custody, and handoffs. A
resource becomes local stock or a production input only after acceptance.

## Runnable fourteen-month grain loop

The first profile is explicitly **synthetic**. Every quantity is gameplay
calibration, not an estimate of Ming population, yields, military rations, or
prices. Each month selects relief first, force first, balanced allocation, or
military requisition. Winter closes a route, autumn harvest requires a protected
seed floor, and requisition reduces cooperation and the next harvest.

```bash
cargo run -p canwu-economy-reference --example grain_loop
cargo test -p canwu-economy-reference
```

The resource extension remains the sole balance writer. A force submits demand
and a consumption intent instead of debiting grain directly. Transport arrival
is not delivery until custody handoff and acceptance complete. The fixture can
therefore check monthly conservation, retries, restore, and exact replay.

## Ming case: a Songjiang cotton archetype

The historical content is scoped to household cotton-textile production in
Songjiang and the Lower Yangzi, 1450–1644. It supports an organizational
archetype of household spinning and weaving, including households specializing
in particular preparation or production stages, with merchants and brokers
mediating distribution. Some workshops produced higher-grade and fancy cloth.
Separately, large urban workshops handled the final dyeing and calendaring
operations. The sources do not establish a universal Ming workshop size, wage,
productivity level, modern factory coefficient, or empire-wide organization.

The main model card uses Harriet T. Zurndorfer's research on late-imperial
cotton textile manufacture and marketing, with page locators stored in the
content. The source connects Songjiang cotton production with peasant-family
production, commercialization, and distribution networks. The Canwu profile
therefore preserves household and specialist-household organization instead of
rewriting it as an anachronistic concentrated factory.

## China industrialization case: Hanyang–Daye–Pingxiang

The second historical archetype is explicitly a **Middle Yangzi Hubei–Jiangxi
industrial chain**, not a “North China industrial corridor”:

- Hanyang Iron Works was in Hubei;
- the Daye iron mine was in Hubei;
- Pingxiang coal was in Jiangxi; the 1896 evidence supports the mining bureau's
  acquisition of mining and railway land, not an already operating route;
- 1896–1907 is modeled as linked but unconsolidated Hanyang, Daye, and Pingxiang
  precursor enterprises; capability evidence is represented as dated nodes—an
  1896 survey/land observation, mechanized adits operating by 1904, and a route
  that reached Liling in 1903 and extended to Zhuzhou during 1905. Because the
  fixture uses whole years, 1906 is the first full-year proxy for rail movement
  to Zhuzhou and onward riverboat movement toward Hubei; intervening capability
  is not inferred;
- only 1908–1911 uses the consolidated Hanyeping organizational class, while
  actual route accessibility remains an independently evidenced condition.

Its model cards cite Yun Liu's study of the Hanyeping company's formation,
Jeff Hornibrook's research on mechanized mining, local elites, and mining land
in Pingxiang, Jiangxi, Elizabeth Perry's chronology of the modern mine and the
rail-to-river supply chain, and Shellen Xiao Wu's evidence for water transport
before the railway and for an operating mine in 1905.
The case tests how mineral capability, extraction
processes, interregional routes, energy, facilities, and enterprise organization
jointly constrain production. It cannot be generalized into national
representativeness, inevitable industrialization, automatic technology
diffusion, or a universal productivity coefficient.

## Military and civilian-economy interaction

Food, fodder, physical currency, ammunition, spares, and fuel remain distinct
resources rather than one universal “supply score.” Shortage produces
force-local readiness, fatigue, cohesion, disease, or desertion consequences.
Civilian welfare, cooperation, and future-production effects cross into the
economy integration as typed requisition intents. The economy applies or
rejects them at an expected revision, then the force acknowledges the outcome,
so neither package writes the other's state.

## Scarcity and price

`LocalScarcityProjection` explains the stock, demand, buffers, route access,
security, and policy actually observable to one holder. It does not claim a
price. `PricePressureProjection` is materialized only when the same scope and
interval has executed, quoted, administered, or contracted price evidence plus
a revisioned interpretation rule. Neither projection creates money, moves
goods, transfers ownership, or settles trade.
