This case connects communication demand to physical delivery. The information
ledger remains transport-neutral: the `canwu-correspondence` domain extension
resolves an address, plans from the sender's own holder ledger, persists
transport execution, and advances the addressed delivery through canonical
ingress.

## Run it

```shell
cargo run -p canwu-correspondence --example routed_correspondence
```

The example runs two requests through the same contract:

| Request | Accepted itinerary | Arrival |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Wuxi local delivery | one horse leg | minute `30` |
| Wuxi to Beijing | scheduled rail to the Beijing station, then a horse final mile | minute `3060` |

The fixture branches to select local or long-distance input data. The
correspondence plugin itself has no Beijing-specific planning path: recipient
address and network knowledge determine the accepted route.

## Lifecycle

<Steps>
1. An application or channel adapter prepares one single-recipient addressed `Dispatch` and chooses a logical `due_at` deadline.
2. A decision-backed command or a deterministically selected automatic `CommunicationOpportunity` admits the correspondence intent.
3. The current plugin requires the carrier holder to equal the sender, reads only that sender-owned address, endpoint, and connection knowledge, then persists the accepted `RoutePlan` and exact knowledge cut.
4. One atomic information operation activates the dispatch and creates its first `DeliveryAttempt`.
5. `TransportExecution` advances each leg and records an explicit custody `Handoff` at a carrier or mode boundary.
6. On-time final arrival completes the attempt and dispatch through idempotent information operations, preserving exact record-version evidence. A failed or late attempt leaves the dispatch `Active` for explicit recovery.
</Steps>

`RoutePlan.estimated_arrival_at` is an estimate. `DeliveryAttempt.due_at` is the
immutable information deadline. A late arrival marks the `DeliveryAttempt` as
`Failed` and the `CorrespondenceOperation` as `DeadlineMissed`; it does not
rewrite the original deadline.

## Incidents and demand

A disaster uses operation-keyed RNG, fails the current leg, and replans from the
current endpoint. Legs remain append-only and revision-qualified, and every
accepted revision retains its exact knowledge cut and planning evidence. The
same delivery attempt continues under the successor itinerary revision. The
replacement first leg records custody transfer from the failed leg, including
failure-time and delivery-attempt evidence. Incidents that are not applicable
to an active transport attempt are retained as suppressed evidence without
mutating the operation state.

While the current attempt is `WaitingForRoute` and transport is
`ReplanPending`, the sender-authorized `resolve_correspondence_v1` command can
replan that same attempt after knowledge changes. After terminal failure, the
same command can create a contiguous successor `DeliveryAttempt` with a fresh
`TransportExecution` and deadline, or explicitly finalize the failed dispatch.
Failure alone leaves the dispatch `Active` and never rewrites the prior
attempt's deadline.

Interception creates an `Access` record for the interceptor and does not
automatically stop delivery. Destruction, decoding, redirection, or political
effects require separate domain rules.

Automatic demand is bounded and deterministic. One operation-keyed draw decides
whether an opportunity occurs; another selects one recipient. The opportunity
is consumed when correspondence starts, so snapshot restore cannot choose a
different recipient.

## Historical and modern channels

Ancient post roads, 1900/1940 rail, modern rail, air, telegraph, and other signal
networks can share time-dependent route planning. Their adapters still own
period-specific transfers, operating hours, booking rules, security, encoding,
and final-mile behavior. A telegraph route, for example, can combine office
collection, signal transmission, and physical delivery without pretending
that the signal leg is a train journey.

Route search does not reserve scarce capacity. The current request contract has
only `CorrespondenceCapacityAdmission::Unconstrained`; the plugin neither
checks nor persists a booking. Rail, air, courier, or signal capacity needs a
future admission variant carrying exact `CapacityBooking` or simulation
reservation evidence. This example therefore demonstrates only unconstrained
delivery.

The tests restore snapshots and replay the full operation exactly. They also
cover post-handoff rerouting without losing prior legs, route recovery after a
knowledge update, successor-attempt retry, explicit finalization, external
progress rejection, and holder-read authority. Persisted evidence includes the
demand decision, per-revision knowledge cuts, routes, handoffs, incidents,
recovery commands, information operations, and exact dispatch/attempt versions.
